# Roles and responsibilities

Every person signs in with a role. The role decides which pages appear in the sidebar and what you can do on them. Administrators can adjust roles in **Administration → Roles & Permissions**. Below, each role has a short quick start: what you can do, where to go, and what to do first.

## Super Administrator

You can do everything, including managing roles, users and settings. Permission checks never block you.

### Your pages
Every module, plus Administration: Users, Roles & Permissions, Counties, Settings, Website Inquiries and Audit Logs.

### First steps
- Create staff accounts under **Users** and give each person the narrowest role that fits.
- Review **Settings** (company details, default delivery charge, forecasting options).
- Check **Roles & Permissions** to confirm each role matches how your team works.
- Review **Audit Logs** regularly to see who changed what.

## Administrator

You manage the whole system day to day, with the same access as the Super Administrator except that only a Super Administrator can create or change the Super Administrator role.

### Your pages
All modules and all Administration pages.

### First steps
- Add and deactivate users; assign roles.
- Add counties and sub-counties as you expand.
- Read **Website Inquiries** and approve farmers who registered on the website.
- Manage search-engine optimisation and website content under **SEO & Site Content**.

## CEO

You have read-only access to every module so you can follow the whole business without changing anything.

### Your pages
Dashboard, all supply chain, commerce, logistics, finance, reports, intelligence, and the administration lists (view only).

### First steps
- Start on the **Dashboard**: revenue, procurement, gross profit, outstanding payments, deliveries.
- Use **Analytics** and **Market Intelligence** for trends, wastage, profitable commodities and payment risks.
- Open **Reports** for the financial performance and county/centre performance reports.

Remember: gross profit is goods revenue less the cost of stock sold; it is not net profit.

## Operations Manager

You run supply chain, orders and logistics day to day.

### Your pages
Farmers, Suppliers, Cooperatives, Collection Centres, Products, Procurement, Inventory, Quality Control, Customers, Orders, Sales, Vehicles, Transporters, Dispatch, Deliveries, Routes, Reports, Analytics and Forecasting. Finance pages are view-only for you.

### Daily routine
1. Check the Dashboard for pending orders, low stock and late deliveries.
2. Confirm new orders; where stock is short, make sure the procurement request is being worked.
3. Watch Dispatch and Deliveries so nothing is late.
4. Use **Demand Forecasting** to plan next week's purchasing.

## Procurement Officer

You find produce and buy it from farmers and suppliers.

### Your pages
Farmers, Suppliers, Cooperatives, Procurement (create and manage), plus view access to Products, Centres, Inventory and Quality Control, and the Forecasting and Market Intelligence pages.

### Daily routine
1. Open **Procurement** and look at requests, especially ones created automatically for customer orders.
2. Assign a farmer or supplier (use **Market Intelligence** to see who ranks best for the commodity), then **Confirm purchase** with the agreed price.
3. When produce arrives, **Record produce received**. Quality Control will then grade it.
4. Review **Demand Forecasting → Procurement recommendations** and raise purchases for urgent items.
5. Approve new farmers who registered on the website (Farmers → ⋯ → Approve registration).

## County Coordinator

You look after farmers and suppliers in your area.

### Your pages
Farmers and Suppliers (manage), Counties, Collection Centres, Procurement and Products (view), Reports.

### Daily routine
- Register farmers, assign them to their nearest collection centre, and record the commodities they grow.
- Record **harvest schedules** on each farmer's profile so procurement can plan ahead.
- Follow up pending website registrations and activate those you have verified.
- Use the Farmers report and county performance report to see where supply is strong or weak.

## Aggregation Centre Officer

You receive produce at a collection centre.

### Your pages
Farmers (manage), Procurement (manage), and view access to Collection Centres, Inventory, Quality Control and Products.

### Daily routine
1. Open **Procurement** and find confirmed purchases due at your centre.
2. When the farmer delivers, open the procurement and **Record produce received** with the real quantities.
3. Hand over to Quality Control for grading — only accepted produce becomes stock and is paid for.
4. Check **Inventory** for what is on hand at your centre.

## Logistics Manager

You move orders from the centres to customers.

### Your pages
Vehicles, Transporters, Dispatch, Deliveries and Routes (manage), plus view access to Orders, Customers, Collection Centres and Reports.

### Daily routine
1. Open **Dispatch → New dispatch**; select orders that are *Ready for dispatch*, assign vehicle, driver, route and times, and enter the transport cost.
2. Click **Depart** when the vehicle leaves.
3. Update each **Delivery** as it happens: Delivered (with recipient and proof) or Failed/Returned (with a reason — the order returns to Ready so it can be rescheduled).
4. Keep vehicle availability and insurance/inspection dates up to date.

## Finance Manager

You handle money in and out.

### Your pages
Invoices, Payments, Expenses and Supplier Settlements (manage), Financial Transactions, Reports, plus view access to Customers, Orders and Procurement.

### Daily routine
1. **Payments:** record customer payments against open invoices (cash, bank, M-Pesa with its code). Reverse a payment if it was entered wrongly.
2. **Supplier Settlements:** pay farmers and suppliers for accepted produce; partial payments are allowed.
3. **Expenses:** record operating costs; void instead of deleting.
4. Review **Financial Transactions** (the ledger) and the *receivables* and *liabilities* reports.

## Sales Manager

You manage customers and their orders.

### Your pages
Customers (manage), Orders (create and manage), Marketplace, Sales, Invoices and Payments (view), plus Products, Inventory, Reports, Forecasting and Website Inquiries.

### Daily routine
1. Register wholesale and institutional customers with payment terms and credit limits.
2. Create orders for them (or order on their behalf through the Marketplace) and **Confirm** to allocate stock.
3. Answer **Website Inquiries** and mark them handled.
4. Use **Sales** and **Market Intelligence** to see top sellers and customers with overdue invoices.

## Warehouse Manager

You keep stock accurate.

### Your pages
Inventory and Collection Centres (manage), plus view access to Products, Procurement, Quality Control, Orders and Logistics.

### Daily routine
1. Check **Inventory → Batches** for expiry dates; write off damaged or expired produce with a reason.
2. **Transfer** stock between centres and warehouses.
3. After a stock count, **Adjust count** (a reason is required).
4. Watch low-stock alerts and tell Procurement.

Every change is permanent in the movement history; corrections are new movements, never edits.

## Quality Control Officer

You grade incoming produce.

### Your pages
Quality Control (manage), plus view access to Procurement, Inventory and Products.

### Daily routine
1. Open **Quality Control → Awaiting inspection**.
2. Click **Inspect**. For each commodity choose a grade (A, B, C or Rejected), the accepted quantity, comments and an optional photo.
3. Save. Accepted produce enters stock and becomes payable to the supplier; rejected quantity does not.
4. Use the history and acceptance rate to see which suppliers deliver the best quality.

## Content & SEO Manager

You look after how Village City appears in search engines and social media, and the words and FAQs on the public website.

### Your pages
**SEO & Site Content** (Administration), plus the Dashboard, Website Inquiries and view access to Products.

### Daily routine
1. Open **SEO & Site Content → Overview** and fix any red or amber items in the health check.
2. **Pages:** write a unique title and meta description for each public page and check the Google and social-share previews.
3. **Site content:** update the headlines, hero image, steps and announcement bar on the website.
4. **FAQs:** add the questions buyers and farmers ask most; tick "Show on the home page" for the best ones.
5. **Products & categories:** give each product a clear description and, for best sellers, a custom meta description. Add an introduction paragraph to each category.

## Customer

You buy from Village City.

### Your pages
Marketplace and Orders (your own orders only).

### First steps
1. Open the **Marketplace**, add what you need — the product flies into your cart — then open the cart.
2. Choose delivery address and date, add instructions, and **Place order**.
3. Track it under **Orders**; open an order to see progress and delivery updates.
4. Use **Reorder** to repeat a past order quickly.

See the **Buying on Village City** guide for details.

## Farmer

You supply produce to Village City.

### Your pages
Help & Documentation and your profile. Farmers do not have operational pages yet: the Village City team records your deliveries, grading and payments for you.

### How it works
- Register at the website's **Sell with us** page, or ask a Village City officer to register you. The team confirms your details and links you to a collection centre.
- When buyers order, procurement officers contact you with the quantity and agreed price.
- Deliver to your collection centre. Produce is graded; **you are paid for the accepted quantity** at the agreed price.
- Ask your county coordinator or collection centre officer for your delivery and payment history.
